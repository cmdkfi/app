# Discrepancies

1. **The advertised one-unit migration guarantee is stronger than the checked aggregate bound.**  
   README lines 25 and 49 promise at most one loan-token unit of rounding. The web app repeats **0.000001 USDC** in `Choose.tsx:57`, `Settings.tsx:64`, and `Position.tsx:133`; landing content repeats it at `src/content/landing.ts:588`. The executor checks destination debt against transferred assets plus one, while its invariant and example tests allow **two units across both markets**. This is a documentation/verification discrepancy; I did not demonstrate a live two-unit increase or an exploit.

2. **The web app incompletely explains protective sales below the act line.**  
   `app/src/screens/Settings.tsx:36,66` associates sales below 70% with the 20% discretionary budget and explains unbudgeted protection as occurring above 70%. In `Executor.sol:258–266`, a protective session can continue below 70%, for up to 24 hours, while LTV remains above target plus tolerance. Such sales remain exempt from the discretionary budget. The protocol README explains this correctly; the UI needs the same qualification.

3. **The mainnet signer count is stale.**  
   README lines 53 and 88 say **17 operators on mainnet**. A read-only `state` query to `v1.signer` returned **16 participants**, threshold **11**, during this screening. Testnet’s stated 11-of-16 matched its live state.

4. **The product narrative is broader than the current guided flow.**  
   README describes choosing LTV/corridor settings; the deployed app fixes 50% target, 70% act, 30% floor, and 20% budget. Its own README and landing caption disclose those presets. Likewise, BTC, Intents, private funding, other venues, and relevering are future features, not delivered features deserving current implementation credit.

5. **Some verification procedures cannot literally be run under read-only rules.**  
   Step 7’s `--land` command sends transactions and overwrites evidence. Vault sandbox tests create accounts and sign. Their recorded evidence can be inspected, but should not be presented as a fresh execution by this screening. All actual Verify-it scripts and referenced evidence paths existed.

6. **Some research and design references are not self-contained.**  
   References including `incident_replay/`, `backtest_scripts/incident_replay_llm.py`, and `research/liquidation-backtest/backtest_scripts/rpc.py` were absent. `Executor.sol:7` refers to absent `docs/01-design-and-feasibility.md`. `research/README.md:15–25` explains that some references point to internal planning material and that substantial raw inputs/model responses are not included. This disclosure does not make those experiments independently reproducible from the checkout.

7. **Research headline numbers have different levels of confirmation.**
   - The included count table sums to **20,479 liquidations**. Supplied result tables contain **1,890/1,895** and **5,009/5,011** avoided observed liquidations.
   - I did **not** reconstruct the underlying chain history or rerun the broad replay/grid. The reported cohort also has venue/feed/size and reconstruction qualifications. Its tables include policy-caused liquidations of previously surviving positions; the headline avoidance ratio is not an all-outcomes safety rate.
   - The **20-position / $2.65M** result, idealized **$8,811 versus $5,987 / approximately $2,800** comparison, incident-replay advantage, **44 hostile items / 21-of-30 versus 2-of-30** results, repeated-answer variability, and retrieval/ML comparisons were not independently regenerated. Some have supporting tables, but that is weaker than a reproducible experiment.
   - Large research-release assets and internal raw caches were not downloaded and replayed.

8. **Operational and business numbers remain unconfirmed.**  
   I did not independently establish the README’s borrower/adoption totals, historical liquidation totals, cited external incident losses, or market-size estimates. The **$0.70–$1.00 per vault/month** and **2–3 cents per action** are estimates, not measurements of the current two-second scheduling configuration. Historical UI performance claims were not benchmarked with a real wallet in this run.

9. **Transcript verification has a narrower trust claim than “independent attestation verification.”**  
   The verifier checks recorded bytes, hashes, signature, signer binding, and recorded TCB status. It trusts the agent’s recorded model-attestation verdict; it does not independently reverify the model’s TDX quote, GPU attestation, or a pinned model measurement. The README discloses this. I checked three transcript samples, not all 690 records, and did not independently recount every advertised failure category.

10. **Several limitations are disclosed and should remain visible to judges.**  
    The deployment is unaudited, testnet liquidity and demo feeds are controlled demonstrations, price-gap protection demonstrably fails, and the price scenes do not show material AI improvement over the deterministic keeper. Owner/position linkage and decision transcripts are public in this build. None should be silently converted into a production-readiness claim.

# Preliminary score: **8/10**

This is a screening assessment, not an official judging rubric or eligibility decision.

The strongest evidence is the working cross-chain deployment, restricted NEAR authority, code-enforced executor policy, exact agent-image reproduction, independently corroborated historical actions and reverts, and a web app whose build matches deployment and passes substantial tests.

The remaining gap to a higher score is the uncompleted vault source rebuild, inconsistent safety wording, incomplete research reproducibility, and limited independently established benefit from the model over deterministic protection. The environment-blocked vault build is **not** treated as a demonstrated project failure.
