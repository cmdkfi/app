# cmdk — borrow against your crypto without selling it, guarded by an agent that cannot move your money

> NEAR HACKATHON, 1–14 November 2026. Tracks: Unstoppable DeFi, AI Security & Defense, Privacy & Private AI. The build runs on NEAR testnet and Base Sepolia; unaudited; mainnet after an audit.

## What it does

You sign in with the wallet you already have and deposit the ETH or BTC you want to borrow against, from any chain, through Confidential Intents. It becomes collateral on Morpho Blue on Base with a USDC credit line at the LTV you choose, and you move the USDC to your wallet or to a supported chain with one signed message. If you start from stablecoins, they can be swapped into the collateral first.

**What the hackathon build runs.** NEAR testnet and Base Sepolia, with WETH deposited directly on Base Sepolia. NEAR Intents and Confidential Intents have no testnet, so any-chain and private deposits and withdrawals ship in the first production release and are not demonstrated here. Of the rest, the live evidence covers registration, the protective `reduce`, the `migrate`, revocation, `close` and `withdraw` (steps 6 to 12); the agent's `exit` and the owner's `open_from_stables` have executor and vault tests but no live transaction in the evidence.

An AI agent watches the position and the market around the clock and acts inside a corridor you set: it can sell some collateral early when risk rises, move the position to another market you approved when its market shows stress, or exit if no approved market can take it (buying back after a recovery is a production-release item, see below). A vault on NEAR checks every proposal against your policy and signs one call; an immutable executor on Base re-reads Morpho's oracle and your position and refuses anything outside your corridor. You hold no new keys. Everything the agent can trigger pays back into your own position; money leaves only through a withdrawal your wallet signs.

## Three layers

**Signal.** The agent reads everything and may propose one of three actions: `reduce` (sell collateral to repay debt), `migrate` (move the position, or part of it, to another market you approved: same collateral, the same debt up to one loan-token unit of rounding, no sale), `exit` (repay everything and take the collateral out of the market). It supplies no prices, no calldata, no recipient, no number anyone must trust. It can never increase your debt: buy-back after a recovery (`relever`) is a production-release action, not in this build, because in our backtest it kept the same positions alive and ended with less equity.

**Authorization.** The vault on NEAR decides whether the proposal is allowed: right market, within the slice maximum, cooldown elapsed, agent registered and not revoked. It decides whether an owner message is genuine. It builds and signs exactly one executor call through NEAR Chain Signatures, with a 10-minute deadline. It never computes a price.

**Execution.** The executor on Base decides whether the action is inside your corridor right now, using data only Base has: Morpho's market oracle and the age of its Chainlink feeds, the position, the market. It enforces the corridor's lines and budgets, requires swap output of at least the oracle price minus a 1% ceiling, pins every recipient to the position's own address, and refuses expired calls, all in one atomic transaction.

**No backstop in v1.** The hackathon version builds the positive flows. If NEAR, Chain Signatures, the relayer or the agent is down, your position waits until they return. A backstop that needs none of them, a permissionless `protect` call on Base, is on the hardening roadmap.

## Your corridor

The agent's main job is to keep this buffer in calm times, selling small slices early so the position never arrives at a crash close to the line. In the backtest, what saved positions on 10 October 2025 was the distance built days earlier, not reaction speed. Protective selling above the act line is the backstop, not the plan. Vitalik Buterin's April 2026 design for LLM wallets asks a human above a daily limit; we use the corridor and the daily budget instead, because per-action approvals train users to click yes (Willison), and because none of the three actions in this build adds debt: `reduce` and `exit` lower your exposure, `migrate` keeps it.

| Setting | Default | What it bounds |
|---|---|---|
| Target / act line | 50% / 70% | Crossing the act line starts protective selling back to target; it stops at target, including when the price recovers |
| Floor | 30% | The agent never sells below this |
| Daily sell budget | 20% of collateral | Discretionary selling below the act line, rolling 24 hours |
| Agent may migrate | yes | Moves only between markets you set a corridor for; at most two a day |
| Agent may exit | yes | Whether the agent may close the position at all; after an exit, only you can re-open. The agent's code tries `migrate` first while an approved market can take the position; the executor enforces only this switch |

The production release adds buy-back (`relever`) with a ceiling LTV, a daily buy budget and a hold time after a sale. This build has none of them.

**What the model adds, and how we measure it.** The agent's numeric screens are code: LTV against the act line, collateral worth zero at the oracle, utilization above 95%, and a stale or negative feed. Borrow rate, pool liquidity and the gap between the oracle and the pool price are read every loop as inputs to the judge and to execution, not as alarms. The model gets those numbers plus text a rule cannot read (in this build Base's status feed and two demo feeds we publish ourselves, labelled as ours in `feeds/README.md`; protocol, security and news sources are a production-release item) and chooses among fixed options: sell early inside your budget, move to another approved market, exit if none can take it. In our incident replay (8 incidents, 6 calm windows, mostly supplier and depositor positions; see Limitations; the model run with thinking on at every step, as in production) numeric rules alone exited in time in 6 of 8; the model with the same numbers plus text also in 6 of 8, earlier in 5 of them (136 minutes versus 25 before the point of no return in the widest case, 14 minutes in the narrowest) and at the same minute in the sixth; text alone in 3 of 8. Each had one false exit in the 6 calm windows. The deterministic twin in the scenes runs the same numeric screens without the model, so the difference is what the model adds. In the three price scenes of step 10 the two ended within a unit of each other: the numeric screens did that work, as the replay also found for detection. The model's contribution in the live build is the venue-stress `migrate` of step 10, where the judge named the market itself as the problem, and the earlier exits in the replay; the full results table is `research/judge-evidence/incident-replay-results.md`. The executor decides what is allowed; the model decides what is wise.

**Worst case for a fully compromised agent.** What the contracts enforce, per day: discretionary sales up to the sell budget, each filled no worse than 1% below Morpho's oracle; up to two moves between markets you approved, costing gas only; one exit per approved market with a position (an exit closes that market for the agent); and at most 16 discretionary sales per collateral token in the window. A `reduce` cannot sell below your floor; an `exit` repays all debt and returns the remaining collateral to your position's address. It cannot increase your debt (a move may add one loan-token unit of rounding), send tokens anywhere but your position's own address, or act more than 10 minutes after you revoke it. Protective selling is not budget-limited: a protective session starts only when the oracle LTV is above the act line, continues for at most 24 hours while the LTV stays above target plus a tolerance, and each slice repays towards target; a fill better than the oracle can leave the LTV a fraction of a point below target. What that can cost you is more than the execution cost: a sale or an exit gives up exposure (an exit at 50% LTV sells about half your collateral), and the 1% bound is against the oracle, which can itself sit a few percent from the exchange price during a crash. The contracts bound what the agent can do, not what the market does afterwards.

## Why NEAR

The vault is one immutable contract per user that controls the Base address through NEAR's Chain Signatures network, the same network Omni Bridge uses to sign outbound transfers. It verifies the agent's enclave attestation on-chain and gives the agent a credential the NEAR protocol itself limits to three named methods (`reduce`, `migrate`, `exit`), accepts owner actions from an existing EVM wallet with no new keys, and pins the agent's code on-chain. NEAR Intents bring deposits from any supported chain and deliver to any (production release; Intents has no testnet). A Base-only version could exist; what NEAR adds is a credential scoped by the chain itself, on-chain attestation, one policy and one revocation across every venue on the roadmap, and chain-abstracted in and out. The trade: agent and owner actions reach Base through NEAR and Chain Signatures (a threshold of 11 operators out of 16 on testnet, read from `v1.signer-prod.testnet` on 5 Nov, and 17 on mainnet; the same MPC network NEAR Intents relies on), so if either is unavailable they wait.

## How it works

```
ETH / BTC (or stables), any chain ──Confidential Intents──▶ derived Base address (vault-controlled)
   (hackathon build: WETH sent directly on Base Sepolia; Intents has no testnet)
                                                       │
                     Executor on Base (immutable): reads oracle, position, your corridor;
                     open · reduce · migrate · exit · repay · close; recipients = caller; deadline
                                                       ▲
   vault on NEAR: immutable code, owner = your wallet, allowlist, slice max, cooldown, attestation
   builds, decodes and signs each Base call (Chain Signatures)
                                                       ▲
              reduce · migrate · exit, requested by the agent key
                                                       ▲
              agent in a TDX enclave ─── NEAR AI Cloud (attested model)
```

**The vault** is a NEAR account whose owner is your wallet address. The factory, itself an immutable contract with no keys and the vault code hash fixed inside it, creates the vault with no keys at all and attaches code that is a Global Contract referenced by hash, in one batch, so nobody, including us, can change the code, add a key, or create vaults with other code under the factory. Anyone can call the factory. Owner actions (setup, corridor, open, repay, close, withdraw, approve and revoke the agent) are messages signed by your wallet, relayed by anyone and verified by the vault on-chain with a nonce and expiry. v1 supports externally owned EVM wallets.

**The agent's key.** The agent generates a fresh key inside the enclave; the enclave's hardware quote carries that key. The agent registers by sending the quote to the vault, which verifies it on-chain (Intel's signatures, firmware up to date, debug mode off, the code measurement you approved) and only then adds a function-call key limited to `reduce`, `migrate` and `exit`, valid for 7 days. A restart means a new key and a new registration; changing the code needs your approval.

**The executor** is a small immutable contract on Base with no admin. Anyone may call it, but it acts only on the caller's own position and sends tokens only back to the caller; for you, the caller is your vault's derived address. Its oracles are Morpho's own per-market oracles, built on Chainlink. A stale feed refuses new risk (open) but never refuses selling or exit, because Morpho liquidates on that same price. Its Morpho callback accepts calls only from Morpho, which calls back only the contract that started the repay, the same check Morpho's audited PreLiquidation contract uses.

**The agent** runs in a Phala TDX enclave. Each loop: collect (position, market, oracle and feed age, pool liquidity and a second price every loop, about every two seconds, because an oracle can be wrong while no price falls: Aave's wstETH CAPO incident of March 2026 liquidated about $27M across 34 accounts on a bad parameter, and only an oracle-versus-market check would have caught it; the allowlisted text feeds every minute: in this build Base's status feed and two demo feeds we publish ourselves, labelled as ours in `feeds/README.md`), compute (LTV, distances, amounts, slippage, volatility, in code), screen (rules), judge (the model in NEAR AI Cloud, called when a screen turns on, when a new feed item arrives and once every six hours, thinking on, in its own task so a protective sale never waits for it, since a judgment takes minutes; verified with a fresh-nonce attestation and a model-enclave signature, answering only with a risk level, one of four actions and the one market the judgment is about, by its label (live, backup or demo; `none` only with hold); code applies a judgment to that market and no other), decide (code maps the action to an amount and clamps it to your corridor), propose. A discretionary sale, move or exit needs the judge plus a fired on-chain screen on that market, or the judge plus two text sources with different feed ids (on testnet two of the three feeds are ours, so this path is a demonstration, not independent corroboration). Two things we built and measured are not in the judge's path: a risk model trained on our dataset of 20,479 Morpho liquidations added no alerts to the fixed screens on the 2026 holdout, and feeding the judge the most similar historical cases made exits no earlier and raised false high-risk flags, so those cases are shown to you on the dashboard instead. The model never calculates an amount. Fetched text is treated as untrusted: `exit` needs on-chain confirmation or two independent sources. If the model or its attestation is unavailable, deterministic rules run and are labelled as such. Every judge call writes a transcript with source URLs and content hashes.

## Verify it

Everything runs on NEAR testnet and Base Sepolia. For AI judges: every step below has a read-only check (view calls, the files under `evidence/` and the transcripts the enclave serves) that needs no transaction. Humans with a testnet wallet can run the scripts, which print the exact text quoted. Addresses are in `deployments/testnet.json`: `<vault>` is `near.vault`, `<executor>` is `base.executor`, the market ids are under `base.markets`. NEAR reads use the `near` CLI; Base reads use Foundry's `cast` with `RPC=https://base-sepolia-rpc.publicnode.com`.

1. `near account list-keys <vault> network-config testnet now` → exactly one key once the agent has registered (none before), a function-call key whose methods are `reduce`, `migrate`, `exit`; no full-access keys. `near contract call-function as-read-only <vault> get_state json-args '{}' network-config testnet now` → `owner` is your wallet address (lower case) and `base_address` is the derived Base address. The factory has created vaults beyond the team's `v1` and `v2` (anyone can create one through the relayer, as a tester did with `v3` on 5 Nov); each vault's owner is in its own `get_state`.
2. `near account view-account-summary <vault> network-config testnet now` → `Global Contract (by Hash: SHA-256 checksum hex)` followed by the published hash, also in `deployments/testnet.json` (`near.vault_code_hash_hex`; `near.vault_code_hash` is the same hash in base58). From a clean checkout of the commit the vault was built from (the `link` in the wasm's NEP-330 build info, `https://github.com/cmdkfi/app/tree/<commit>`), made in a directory outside any other git repository, with Docker running: `./scripts/reproduce-hash.sh --reproducible --vault <vault>` → the same hash built from this repo. The script runs `cargo near build reproducible-wasm` (cargo-near 0.22.0) in the image `sourcescan/cargo-near:0.21.1-rust-1.96.0`, pinned by digest in `vault/Cargo.toml`; it prints `sha256 (hex)` equal to the account summary's hash, `sha256 (base58)` equal to the account's `global_contract_hash` on the `published` line, then `result           MATCH`. The factory itself (`factory.prime-measure-1792736012.testnet`, code SHA-256 hex `5315458701e4f52c9c033d05d492ec75d18076a128a0261f153434670971c863`) was built on the host with `cargo near build non-reproducible-wasm`, not in the pinned image, so its NEP-330 metadata carries no link or build info; its source, `vault/factory/`, is unchanged since commit `52e0446`, and rebuilding it there with cargo-near 0.22.0 and Rust 1.95.0 on macOS arm64 gave the same 152,851-byte wasm and hash on 5 Nov (another toolchain or OS may not). What the immutability argument needs is its state: no access keys, and `get_config` returning the vault hash above.
3. `get_state` (step 1) → `markets`, each with its `id`, `executor`, `slice_max` and `corridor_set`, and `cooldown_secs` (120 s between agent calls on NEAR; the executor applies its own 60 s cooldown per position, `cooldownSeconds` in `deployments/testnet.json`). `cast call <executor> "corridors(address,bytes32)(uint64,uint64,uint64,uint64,bool,bool,bool)" <base_address> <live market id> --rpc-url $RPC` → seven lines: target, act line, floor and sell budget in 1e18 units, then migrate allowed, exit allowed and corridor set, that is `500000000000000000 [5e17]`, `700000000000000000 [7e17]`, `300000000000000000 [3e17]`, `200000000000000000 [2e17]`, `true`, `true`, `true`. The same call with the backup market's id → your corridor there. The limit of two moves a day is a constant of the executor (`MAX_MOVES`), not a corridor field.
4. `./scripts/ltv.sh <vault> --market backup` (the team vault's position moved from live to backup in the venue-stress scene of step 10 on 5 Nov; without `--market` the script reads live and prints `(no position on this market)`) → collateral, debt and LTV computed with Morpho's own share math (debt = `borrowShares` × (`totalBorrowAssets` + 1) / (`totalBorrowShares` + 1e6), rounded up) and the market's oracle (address in `deployments/testnet.json`, reading Chainlink ETH/USD `0x4aDC67696bA383F43DD60A9e78F2C97Fbbfc7cb1`) at 1e36 scale, in two columns. "at lastUpdate" equals the executor's `ltvOf` view (the script ends with `cross-check    MATCH with the "at lastUpdate" LTV (WAD)`). "accrued to block <n>" adds the interest since Morpho's `lastUpdate`; `reduce` calls Morpho's `accrueInterest` first, so that is the figure the policy acts on in a transaction; the two differ by the interest since `lastUpdate`.
5. `cast call 0xBBBBBbbBBb9cC5e90e3b3Af64bdAF62C37EEFFCb "isAuthorized(address,address)(bool)" <base_address> <executor> --rpc-url $RPC` → `true` for the executor in `get_state`. `cast call <executor> "config(bytes32)(((address,address,address,address,uint256),address,address[2],uint32[2]))" <live market id> --rpc-url $RPC` → the market's parameters, then its fixed pool `0x57183717A087d2fe3Ad890873877244c3B96156c` (the public Uniswap v3 USDC/WETH 0.01% pool, `base.markets.live.pool`), then its two Chainlink feeds and their maximum ages `[1200, 86400 [8.64e4]]`; the backup market's id gives the same pool.
6. `get_state` → `approved` (the measurement you approved) and `agent` (the registered key, `public_key`, and its `expires_at`; a key is valid 7 days and the agent re-registers itself 24 hours before expiry, so `expires_at` moves forward without an owner action); the `register_agent` transaction on NEAR shows the on-chain quote verification. With the boot's evidence files (`tdx_quote.hex`, `event_log.json`, `vm_config.json`, `app-compose.json`) in `evidence/<boot>/` and Docker running: `./scripts/p3_reproduce_measurements.sh <work dir> evidence/<boot> dstack-0.5.12 <cpu_count> <memory>` (the two values from `vm_config.json`, for example `1 2G`) → `os_image_hash MATCH`, the quote's MRTD and RTMR0–2 next to the values `dstack-mr` computes from the published dstack OS image, RTMR3 next to the replay of the event log, each marked `MATCH`, then `ALL MATCH`; `sha256sum evidence/<boot>/app-compose.json` → the compose hash in the event log (`compose-hash`) and in the quote's MRCONFIGID after its leading `01`.

The agent key's limits (its method list, its receiver and the slice maximum) are not a step here, because no key outside the enclave can sign as the agent and the vault has no other key; they are covered by the vault's test suite, `cargo test` in `vault/` after building the three wasms the sandbox tests load (`(cd mock-signer && cargo near build non-reproducible-wasm) && cargo near build non-reproducible-wasm && (cd factory && cargo near build non-reproducible-wasm)`; 15 unit and 8 sandbox tests), and by the sandbox transcript in `scripts/ATTACKS.md`.

Run by the team from its test wallet, with all model-side checks bypassed: `./scripts/attack-corridor.sh --land 2>&1 | tee evidence/attacks.txt` → one executor revert per line, in this order: `attack Expired: revert Expired 0x203d82d8 PASS` (a signed call broadcast after its deadline), `attack MoveLimit: revert MoveLimit 0x013da4fb PASS` (a third move within 24 hours), `attack UnapprovedMarket: revert UnapprovedMarket 0x99b2f06d PASS` (a move to a market without your corridor), `attack BelowFloor: revert BelowFloor 0x4e7e2916 PASS`, `attack SellBudget: revert SellBudget 0x397b9774 PASS`, then `result: 5 PASS, 0 FAIL`. The executor keys every check on `msg.sender`, so the script uses the wallet's own executor position, the same code path the vault's derived address takes. Recorded in `evidence/attacks.txt`; `scripts/ATTACKS.md` also has a run on a local fork, which sends nothing to the network.

The agent's transcripts, one JSON file per decision loop and watched vault, are public in this testnet build (decided 5 Nov; private to the vault's owner or not served in the production release, see the hardening roadmap): inputs, the model attestation with its fresh nonce and verification result, model id, `provider_tee` signature, the market the judgment names (`judgment_market`), decision. With `E=https://29fc9b0846dc459e21923c5a1b63474d79df6e6b-8080.dstack-pha-prod9.phala.network` (the enclave's evidence endpoint, `AGENT_EVIDENCE_URL` in `cloud/wrangler.jsonc`): `curl -s $E/transcripts` → the index of every transcript on the agent's evidence volume, all boots and vaults, sorted by name, each with `name`, `bytes`, `sha256`, `boot`, `vault` (the vault's tag, `v1` for the factory's vault 1), `twin` and `loop`; `curl -s $E/transcripts | jq -r '.transcripts[].name'` lists the names. `curl -s $E/transcripts/<name> -o /tmp/<name>`, then `./agent/verify-transcript.sh /tmp/<name>` (outside the checkout, so step 2's clean tree stays clean) → one `PASS` line per check, then `ALL PASS <name>`. The verifier recomputes the request and response SHA-256 from the recorded bytes, checks the `provider_tee` signed text, checks that the EIP-191 signer is the address the recorded model attestation names, and requires its recorded TCB status `UpToDate`; it cannot re-verify the model's own TDX quote from a transcript and trusts the attestation verdict the agent recorded. It needs `jq` and Foundry's `cast`, and runs offline on the prototype's enclave transcript, `agent/testdata/transcripts/boot-1793051811-26cc5edf-0001.json`. A loop without a judge call prints `NO-CALL judge outcome: ` with an empty outcome, and a loop whose judge call failed prints `NO-CALL judge outcome: error` (the three such transcripts are from 4 Nov, when the NEAR AI key's spend limit answered HTTP 402; the limit was raised the same day); a loop where no judgment applies records `"applies": "rules only"`.

## Business model

For people who borrow against crypto they want to keep. Coinbase's Morpho-powered loans have about 53,000 borrowers and about $1.56B outstanding (September 2026); Coinbase's own FAQ says it "cannot prevent collateral liquidation on Morpho"; about 3,300 of its borrowers were liquidated in one week of February 2026, and about $394M was liquidated on the Base cbBTC/USDC market between November 2025 and July 2026. Automated protection exists on Base from $500 of debt (DeFi Saver), yet it covers fewer than 1,000 positions, so the gap is trust and distribution, not access. Pricing is not decided: a subscription paid from the owner's wallet, or an annual fee on collateral; either way money leaves the vault only by the owner's signature. Our infrastructure cost per vault is about $0.70–1.00 a month (estimate: chain fees, the enclave's share, inference, agent registration; support, monitoring, security review and paid RPC tiers are not in it): one enclave serves all vaults, the model is called when a screen turns on, on new feed items and once every six hours, and an action costs about 2–3 cents. Details in `research/costs/costs-and-pricing.md`.

## Limitations

- The vault and the executor are new and unaudited, so v1 runs on testnet; the executor follows the callback pattern of Morpho's audited PreLiquidation contract, with fork, fuzz and invariant tests but no outside review before the deadline.
- The model is untrusted by design, and we measured how much. In a replay with 44 hand-written hostile items, fake alerts and "withdraw now" posts made the judge propose an exit on calm windows in 21 of 30 trials; the agreement rule above let 2 of the 30 through. Fake all-clears during real incidents delayed 3 of 8 exits but removed none (`research/judge-evidence/README.md`, with the runs; one model, hand-written items, small windows). The same judge is not reproducible: identical requests gave different answers in up to a third of repeats, so each decision's signed transcript is the record. The agent reads news, governance posts and advisories, which is untrusted input, and the model makers say injection is here to stay: OpenAI, that it is "unlikely to ever be fully 'solved'"; Anthropic, that "a 1% attack success rate … still represents meaningful risk"; Simon Willison, that "once an LLM agent has ingested untrusted input, it must be constrained". So every limit sits in contracts, and the model only chooses among fixed actions.
- With one venue, the vault's own job is small: allowlist, slice cap, cooldown, owner signatures, attestation. Its value is one owner, one agent credential and one revocation across the venues and chains on the roadmap, plus chain-abstracted deposits and withdrawals.
- Chain Signatures signing latency is measured on testnet only; agent and owner actions wait if NEAR or the network is unavailable, and v1 has no backstop meanwhile.
- Protection is risk management, not insurance. A dip that crosses the act line but stops short of liquidation and recovers leaves you about $1,200 per $10k behind an unprotected position with the deterministic rule (same simulation); buy-back after a recovery narrows it in the production release; your corridor sets the trade-off.
- The derived address needs a small ETH float for gas, funded by the service.
- Testnet differences: our own markets; the live and backup markets swap in the public Uniswap v3 USDC/WETH 0.01% pool, kept at the oracle price by our ±1% liquidity band and a script (`scripts/rebalance_pool.py`), and the demo market in a WETH/dUSDC pool on its mock oracle; a scripted liquidator, because Base Sepolia has no liquid markets or liquidators. Intents has no testnet, so the hackathon build takes WETH directly on Base Sepolia and the private funding route is not demonstrated.
- One venue in v1. Rhea on NEAR and Solana are next; a second EVM chain is the same executor with different addresses.
- The venue-stress evidence (our incident replay) comes mostly from supplier and vault-depositor positions; both borrower cases in it were lost to same-block oracle changes that nobody could catch. On Morpho a borrower's collateral is not lent out, so `migrate` here answers a borrow-rate spike or a problem of one market's oracle instance or liquidity, not a lock-up, and the backup market reads the same Chainlink feed. Collateral lock-ups are a problem of pooled venues on the roadmap.
- The owner-to-position link is public; the privacy is in the funding route and the model's inputs.
- The slice maximum on NEAR is 1,000 USDC per market on the team vault, above its 7.7 USDC debt, so in this demo the Base-side corridor is the binding limit; the slice maximum is fixed when the vault is created in this build, and an owner message to change it is a hardening item.
- No admin also means no emergency pause and no in-place bug fix: the remedies are owner actions (revoke, close, `set_executor`) and moving to a new vault version.
- Three edges found by the executor's invariant campaign (2 Nov), left as they are: a `reduce` on a position already above 100% LTV reverts inside Morpho with an arithmetic panic (code 0x11), not with one of the executor's named errors, because the executor never reaches that state through its own calls; a `migrate` of a fraction below about one millionth is refused by Morpho, whose debt rounds up to a whole unit while the collateral rounds down; and a protective slice that fills above the oracle ends below target by an amount that grows with the fill's advantage over the oracle, about 0.36 of a percentage point for a fill 1% better than the oracle from 71% LTV (the repayment formula assumes a fill at the oracle and the executor does not re-check the target after the swap), so "stops at target" means at or below target.

## Hardening roadmap

| Trust that remains | How it is removed |
|---|---|
| One shared enclave serves every vault using ours | Sharding; users running their own agent; two independent agents that must agree before the vault acts |
| Our stack is down (NEAR, Chain Signatures, relayer, agent) | A permissionless `protect(position)` on Base past an emergency line, callable by anyone including you, that may only sell collateral to repay debt |
| We run the enclave and can stop it | Two or three registered agents per vault |
| No emergency stop | An optional guardian chosen by the owner, able only to pause agent actions, never to move funds |
| One TEE vendor | Two agents on different TEE vendors |
| One price source per market | A second price source in the executor; discretionary actions refused when they disagree, protective selling never blocked |
| Text alarms never clear by themselves | A retraction from the same source cancels a text-only alarm, never an on-chain screen |
| One venue | Positions across Morpho, Aave, Kamino and Rhea with caps per venue |
| Transcripts, including the model's reasoning, are public in the build | Private to the vault's owner (an owner-signed read) or not served, in the production release |
| Chain Signatures | Not diversifiable: it is what lets a NEAR vault control addresses on other chains, the same trust NEAR Intents users already accept |

## Where this goes

The credit vault is the first product on a reusable core: a vault you own with no admin keys, an agent bound on-chain to its enclave and limited by the protocol to bounded methods, and an immutable executor per venue chain that re-checks the venue's own data.

The goal is one vault holding each asset where it can be borrowed against, because no single app reaches them all today (checked 21 October 2026, `research/venue-coverage/`):

- NEAR can be lent and borrowed in depth only on NEAR: Rhea Lend, about 3.4M NEAR supplied. In the lending markets we searched (Aave, Morpho, Venus, Kamino and every pool DefiLlama tracks, which includes Compound) there is none.
- Tokenized stocks can be borrowed against mostly on Solana (Kamino, Jupiter Lend, about $60M of stocks posted) and on Base (Aave V4's equities market, non-US), with small markets on Ethereum, BNB Chain and Robinhood Chain, and none on NEAR, although Ondo stock tokens reached NEAR through near.com in September 2026.

Roadmap, in order: the first production release on mainnet after an audit, including deposits and withdrawals from any chain through NEAR Intents and Confidential Intents, and Intents as the vault's cross-chain execution layer, moving collateral and debt between lending markets through atomic swaps while every position stays in an on-chain market the vault controls; Rhea on NEAR; Solana; tokenized stocks with deleveraging before the weekend price freeze; more EVM chains; caps across venues and rebalancing across assets; hedged mode (stock collateral plus a short perpetual), which needs its own security design.

## Prior art

DeFi Saver is the closest design: positions stay in the user's own Safe, bots cannot move funds and run only strategies the user subscribed to, many strategies check the resulting ratio on-chain; about $390M, years live. Automation runs on four EVM chains (Ethereum, Arbitrum, Optimism, Base), with each position managed separately on its own chain, and the code that runs a user's Safe can be repointed by DeFi Saver's multisig after a timelock. Some 2025 AI agent products limit their agents on-chain (Giza's session keys, Brahma's Safe guard, Almanak's Zodiac Roles); hosted chat-to-trade agents (Bankr and similar) and custodial Telegram bots (Bloom, BONKbot) keep a signing credential or the key itself on the vendor's server with off-chain limits; both kinds have been drained (`research/agent-wallets/ai_trading_agent_wallets.md`, Key Question 2). Kamino Multiply and Instadapp Lite run leverage inside one venue's own vaults; Omni Bridge, HOT and Bitte use Chain Signatures for cross-chain execution. OutLayer, a hackathon partner, runs agent code in TDX workers and holds agent keys in an enclave key store with value-based policies; like us it verifies each worker's quote once at registration and then trusts the key made inside the enclave. The difference is who controls what: in our design the vault owner approves the agent's code and no key outside the enclave can act for the agent, while OutLayer's build allowlist, contracts and result-delivery account are managed by its operator (on-chain state checked 22 October 2026). None combines a user-owned immutable vault on a control-plane chain, an agent bound on-chain to an attested enclave and limited to recipient-less methods, an immutable executor that re-verifies every action against the venue's data, and deposit from and delivery to any chain.

Our claim, stated precisely: among the designs we compared, the tightest limits on an AI agent's power over cross-chain funds, audit pending. What we add as a trust assumption is the Chain Signatures network.
